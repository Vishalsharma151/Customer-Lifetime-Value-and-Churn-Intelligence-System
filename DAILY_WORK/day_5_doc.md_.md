# Notebook 08 — Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) Scenarios

## Project Overview

**Project Name:** Customer Lifetime Value and Churn Intelligence System  
**Notebook:** 08 — Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) Scenarios  
**Dataset:** IBM Telco Customer Churn  
**Technology Stack:** Python, Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib

## Objective

The objective of this notebook is to estimate customer contribution value under different financial and retention assumptions.

It combines customer monthly charges with predicted churn probabilities from Notebook 07 to calculate conservative, base, and optimistic CLV scenarios.

Because the available dataset does not contain complete customer profitability and future retention histories, these calculations are assumption-based estimates rather than validated financial forecasts.

## Key Activities

### 1. Load Retention-Prioritization Results

- Load `customer_retention_priority.csv` from Notebook 07.
- Inspect the dataset and verify required columns.
- Identify customer IDs, monthly charges, and predicted churn probabilities.
- Convert relevant columns to numeric types and handle invalid records.

### 2. Define Financial Assumptions

The notebook uses configurable assumptions, including:

- **Contribution margin:** Default 30%.
- **Forecast horizon:** Default 24 months.
- **Monthly discount rate:** Default 1%.

These values are illustrative and should be adjusted when reliable business-specific estimates become available.

### 3. Calculate Monthly Contribution

Estimated monthly contribution is calculated as:

`Estimated monthly contribution = Monthly charges × Assumed contribution margin`

Monthly charges represent revenue, not profit. The contribution margin is an assumption because the dataset does not provide actual customer-level costs.

### 4. Estimate CLV Scenarios

The notebook calculates three scenarios:

- **Conservative CLV:** Uses a lower retention factor.
- **Base CLV:** Uses the default retention-factor assumption derived from the predicted churn probability.
- **Optimistic CLV:** Uses a higher retention factor.

The calculations discount projected monthly contributions over the selected forecast horizon.

The classifier's churn probability is not a calibrated monthly churn hazard. Therefore, the retention factors used here are illustrative planning assumptions rather than validated month-by-month survival estimates.

### 5. Segment Customers by Value and Risk

Customers are grouped into categories such as:

- High value / high risk.
- High value / lower risk.
- Lower value / high risk.
- Lower value / lower risk.

This segmentation can help prioritize customers for further analysis. It does not establish that a retention offer will change a customer's behavior.

### 6. Visualize CLV Results

- Plot the distribution of estimated customer CLV.
- Compare average estimated CLV across value–risk segments.
- Review differences between conservative, base, and optimistic scenarios.

### 7. Export CLV Results

The notebook saves customer-level scenario estimates, segment summaries, and overall metrics for use in Notebook 09.

## Business Value

This notebook helps demonstrate how churn predictions can support customer-value analysis.

Potential applications include:

- Comparing estimated customer contribution values.
- Identifying high-value customers who also have elevated churn risk.
- Exploring the impact of different financial assumptions.
- Providing illustrative customer-value inputs for the ROI simulator in Notebook 09.

## Limitations

- Monthly charges are revenue, not profit.
- Contribution margin, forecast horizon, and discount rate are assumptions.
- Predicted churn probability does not directly provide a customer's exact remaining lifetime.
- The CLV calculations are scenario estimates and are not validated financial forecasts.
- The notebook does not establish causal retention impact or incremental campaign benefit.

For a production-grade CLV model, additional data would be needed, including customer-level costs, acquisition expenses, historical billing information, and longitudinal retention outcomes.
